# Transparency of reporting vs risk of bias — the dividing line

This layer's seven checks read **registry and publisher metadata** and report **transparency
facts**: whether the record was retracted, whether it cites retracted work, when it was registered,
whether and when its registered fields changed, whether its enrollment is actual or planned, and
whether results were posted in time.

None of that is a statement about the study's **methods**. Risk-of-bias instruments (randomization,
blinding, missing data, measurement, selection of the reported result) answer a different question,
and the INSPECT-SR programme reports that trustworthiness screening and Risk of Bias / GRADE
assessments are **empirically unrelated** — *"no relationship was identified between trustworthiness
assessment and Risk of Bias or GRADE"* `[CLAIMED — Step-2 swarm; DOI to be resolved]`.

Because the axes are reported uncorrelated, folding a transparency signal into a bias domain
("registered retrospectively, therefore probably selective reporting") manufactures an inference
the evidence base denies. This tool therefore:

- keeps its own frozen domain vocabulary, disjoint from the bias-axis domain list;
- states the axis boundary in every rendered surface;
- emits **no** composite score, rank, severity, or confidence — a table of per-domain findings,
  each with the specific evidence observed and the specific evidence that would refute it.

## What this layer does not claim

- It does not claim the study is good, bad, biased, or trustworthy.
- It does not claim an edit to a registry record was improper — records are routinely updated
  after completion for administrative reasons; the tool reports the dated fact and stops.
- It does not make legal determinations about reporting obligations; it reports elapsed time.
- It has **NOT YET been EVALUATED** against expert appraisers (see `calibration.md` at the package
  root and the README). Determinism means the same inputs reproduce the same findings; it does not
  mean the findings are correct.
